Project at a Glance

  2. 02Why

    Repetitive tasks, recording errors, inaccurate data, and limited synchronisation can complicate operations and business analysis.

  3. 03Goal

    The project aims to streamline daily work and support more informed business decisions.

Problem and Proposed Solution

Operational difficulties can become decision-making difficulties.

EMPLOYEE PERSPECTIVE

Friction in daily work

The project addresses repetitive tasks, human error, inaccurate data, limited synchronisation, and conventional POS features that do not adequately support specific operational needs.

OWNER PERSPECTIVE

Information that is difficult to analyse

When operational records are unreliable or poorly connected, the problems compound. Owners have a harder time understanding performance and making informed business decisions.

PROPOSED RESPONSE

A more connected POS experience

Kasivo proposes bringing transactions, stock information, reporting, and employee administration into one application, with AI-assisted explanations and recommendations that owners can understand.

Information Architecture

Separate workspaces for staff, owners, and customers.

The sitemap defines the proposed private POS area and a public customer area accessible through a QR code or link without signing in.

Private / Cashier

Transactions and payments, receipts, order queues, kitchen display, attendance, stock checks, promotions, and cashier closing.

Private / Owner

Dashboard, inventory, sales reports, transaction history, payroll and attendance, cash flow, profit and loss, tax reports, promotions, and settings.

Public / Customer

QRIS payment and payment status, digital receipts, order status or queue information, and current promotions.

Research Materials

Two perspectives on the same business.

Separate guides were used for owners and employees. The supplied files are blank interview templates: their answer fields are empty. They document the questions and research approach, rather than interview responses or validated findings. English translations of the prompts are available below.

OWNER GUIDE / 17 PROMPTS

Business decisions and POS experience

Explores business processes, POS history and costs, operational difficulties, stock decisions, potential AI assistance, and trust in recommendations.

Download Original Owner Template ↓
Read the owner interview questions
  1. Could you introduce yourself? What is your name, and are you the owner or an employee here?
  2. Could you walk me through your business process, from welcoming a customer to taking payment?
  3. Have you used other POS systems before? If so, how many have you tried?
  4. If more than one: What differences have you noticed between your previous POS systems and the one you use now? Why do you think they differ?
  5. Have you found any part of using a POS system difficult or confusing?
  6. If yes: Where exactly do you encounter difficulties—for example, entering orders, updating stock, viewing reports, or another task? What causes those difficulties?
  7. Approximately how much does your POS system cost, and do you pay monthly or annually? If applicable: Is there a significant price difference compared with systems you have used before?
  8. Are there any particular factors you consider when dealing with excess stock?
  9. Have you ever run out of a product while customers still wanted to buy it, or had excess stock that did not sell? What usually causes this?
  10. How do you evaluate the business during slow and busy periods?
  11. Which business decisions do you find most difficult to make?
  12. What additional features would help you make decisions?
  13. Are there any other features you think would be useful to add? If the owner is unsure, discuss examples: a product that may run out soon, a busy day requiring more stock, or a product that could be promoted this week. Which would best address the problems mentioned, and why?
  14. Which part of your business would benefit most from AI assistance: sales forecasting, restocking recommendations, promotion recommendations, analysis of popular and unpopular products, or pricing?
  15. If the application offered a recommendation, would you prefer a data-based explanation or a brief suggestion?
  16. What would make you disregard the recommendation or explanation provided?
  17. Do you have any feedback or suggestions about this POS system? Is there anything you would like developed further or reviewed?
EMPLOYEE GUIDE / 11 PROMPTS

Daily workflows and operational friction

Explores everyday cashier tasks, stock availability, time-consuming interactions, recording errors, and the possible usefulness of low-stock alerts.

Download Original Employee Template ↓
Read the employee interview questions
  1. Could you introduce yourself? What is your name, and are you the owner or an employee here?
  2. Could you walk me through the business process, from welcoming a customer to taking payment?
  3. Have you ever run out of a product while customers still wanted to buy it, or had excess stock that did not sell? What usually causes this?
  4. How do you usually identify which products sell best and which attract less interest?
  5. What tasks do you use the POS application for most often?
  6. Are any parts of the application complicated or time-consuming, especially when there are many customers?
  7. Have you ever entered an order incorrectly or recorded a payment incorrectly? What usually causes these mistakes?
  8. Has a customer ever asked whether a product was in stock when you could not quickly check its availability in the application?
  9. How do you usually find out when a product is running low? Do you check manually, check the application, or wait for the owner to tell you?
  10. Would it help if the application automatically displayed an alert such as, “This product is running low; let the owner know”?
  11. Do you have any concerns or suggestions about the POS application that you think should be addressed?
